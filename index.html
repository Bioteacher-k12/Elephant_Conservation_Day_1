1. Four Levels of Wildlife Conservation

Level 1

Individual Level

Personal choices, ethical consumerism (avoiding ivory products), citizen science reporting, and supporting verified conservation charities.

Level 2

Community Level

Village-led crop deterrents (beehive/chili fences), local wildlife scouts, community-owned ecolodges, and local school education.

Level 3

National Level

National parks, government anti-poaching laws, wildlife corridor zoning, and country-wide elephant management policies.

Level 4

Global Level

International treaties (like CITES ivory trade bans), cross-border wildlife corridors, and global NGO research networks.

2. The 8 Conservation Evaluation Criteria

You will score each chosen program out of these 8 criteria in Part 2
1. Anti-Poaching Patrols

Active field patrols monitoring parks and deterring illegal hunting.

2. Ranger & Staff Training

Equipping and training wildlife rangers, ecologists, and local scouts.

3. Local / Native Cooperation

Partnering with local communities so residents benefit from conservation.

4. Wildlife Law Enforcement

Enforcing national wildlife laws, prosecuting trafficking, and regulating trade.

5. Protected Area Designation

Establishing or expanding parks, reserves, and migration corridors.

6. Public Awareness Campaigns

Educating students, communities, and global audiences to reduce demand.

7. Scientific Research

Tracking populations, GPS movement, genetics, and habitat health over time.

8. Extractive Industry BMPs

Best Management Practices minimizing damage from logging, mining, or farming.
